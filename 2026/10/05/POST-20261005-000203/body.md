At first, it feels like a win: faster code, fewer hours, more free time. But the clock doesn’t stop. As Nick Hodges notes in @infoworld, the time saved is quickly filled with more work, or fewer developers. It’s a dance between Parkinson’s Law and the Jevons Paradox: work expands to fill time, and efficiency can paradoxically increase demand.

The initial burst of speed creates a surplus of capacity, which managers rush to exploit. Backlogs get cleared, new features get launched, and complexity rises. The illusion of free time fades fast. What starts as a productivity boost becomes a productivity trap.

AI can’t replace the human element of design and problem-solving. It can only amplify it. The key is to reinvest that time savings into quality, not quantity. Otherwise, the cycle repeats: faster coding, more work, and a deeper spiral of technical debt.

The real question isn’t whether AI makes developers faster. It’s whether we’re ready to use that speed wisely. 🦉 🦀
