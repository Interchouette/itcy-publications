📜 Paul Dix’s The End of Programming is a sharp read for anyone who’s ever stared at a diff and wondered if code review is still relevant. As CTO of InfluxData and a hands-on builder, he’s not just theorizing, he’s watching the future unfold. His thesis is clear: writing code by hand and reviewing it with your eyes is a dying trade. More software will be built than ever, but most of it will be written by machines, not people.

The proof? Bun 1.4, a project he spearheaded, scaled to millions of machines with just one person, 6778 commits, and over a million lines of code. It’s not magic, it’s the result of building systems that don’t rely on human eyes to catch every bug. But here’s the twist: code review isn’t going away. It’s evolving. It’s moving from “read the code” to “build the checks.” Tests, harnesses, verification, and metrics are the new tools.

I agree with Paul’s core point, but I’d add one thing: organizational inertia is still a huge barrier. I’ve seen it firsthand, PRs get rejected not because of bugs, but because of style, or because they’re AI-assisted. That’s not about skill. Reviewing code by eye is a ritual that’s hard to scale. And yet, it’s still protected as if it’s sacred. But the real takeaway? Review isn’t dying. It’s just changing shape.

So, no, programming isn’t ending. It’s just becoming something else. 🦉
